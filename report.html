Indoor Oyster Mushrooms Cultivation Project

A simple production report based on a 6-bucket indoor cultivation sample of white oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus ostreatus).

Sample size: 6 buckets | Bucket volume: 20 L each | Fruiting holes: 16 per bucket | Conditions: 20 to 28 C, 85 to 95 percent RH

Executive Summary

This report summarizes expected production from six identical indoor cultivation buckets of white oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus ostreatus). Under practical growing conditions, the sample can produce approximately 7.2 to 18.0 kg in total, depending on substrate quality, spawn vigor, humidity control, and fresh air exchange.

The first flush usually contributes the majority of the harvest, while later flushes are smaller and gradually taper off as the substrate becomes depleted.

Methodology

Parameter Specification
Species White oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus ostreatus)
Sample size 6 identical buckets
Bucket capacity 20 L per bucket
Substrate Pasteurized straw or similar lignocellulosic substrate
Spawn rate 5 to 10 percent
Fruiting holes 16 well-distributed holes per bucket
Growing conditions 20 to 28 C temperature, 85 to 95 percent relative humidity, adequate fresh air exchange

Experimental Setup

Each bucket was prepared with the same substrate fill, spawn application, and fruiting-hole arrangement. The six buckets were treated as an equal sample for comparing expected productivity under indoor conditions.

  • Uniform bucket volume: 20 L
  • Uniform fruiting holes: 16 per bucket
  • Same substrate and spawn rate across all buckets
  • Indoor fruiting environment with controlled humidity and airflow

Observations

White oyster mushrooms generally respond well to moderate temperatures and high humidity. Good air exchange helps reduce deformation and supports healthy fruit body development. Production is typically highest in the first flush and then declines in subsequent flushes.

In bucket cultivation, performance depends strongly on moisture balance, spawn quality, and the stability of the indoor environment.

Discussion

The wide range between conservative and excellent yield reflects normal variation in substrate preparation, colonization strength, water management, and environmental control. When humidity remains in the preferred range and fresh air exchange is adequate, indoor oyster mushroom performance improves significantly.

For a six-bucket sample, the first flush is the most important harvest event. Later flushes may still be useful, but they usually contribute a smaller proportion of the final yield.

Conclusion

Based on this 6-bucket indoor sample of white oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus ostreatus), the expected total production is approximately 7.2 to 18.0 kg. The report indicates that stable temperature, high humidity, and proper airflow are essential for better output. The charted flush pattern confirms that the majority of harvest comes from the first flush.
